# Gallery Content

## Purpose

Give visitors a quick, visual understanding of the range, craft, and character of Kurt's work without requiring them to enter a long-form case study.

The Gallery appears in the landing page's right pane when the Gallery tab is active. Kurt's concise introduction and the three primary tab controls remain visible in the left pane.

## Content Direction

The gallery should contain a curated collection of public-safe images from Kurt's work. Potential material includes:

- product and interface screenshots
- design explorations and prototypes
- system or architecture diagrams
- developer-tooling surfaces
- process artifacts that communicate how the work developed

The collection should favor strong, distinctive examples over exhaustive coverage. Images may come from projects that also have case studies, but the gallery should feel like a broader visual survey rather than a duplicate case-study index.

## Layout

Arrange the images in a polished, responsive composition that feels intentionally art-directed. The layout may vary image sizes and aspect ratios to create rhythm while preserving clear hierarchy, comfortable spacing, and legibility.

The gallery panel may scroll independently when its content exceeds the viewport. Image loading and responsive sizing should preserve the portfolio's performance goals.

## Desired Visitor Outcome

Visitors should quickly gain confidence in the breadth and quality of Kurt's work and feel invited to explore the deeper case studies.

## Open Questions

- Which work images are available and appropriate for public use?
- Should images include captions, project labels, or links to related case studies?
- Should selecting an image open a larger view or remain a purely browsable composition?
- How should the gallery sequence and group images from different projects?
